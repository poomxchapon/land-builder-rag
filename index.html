Game Design Knowledge Base

The knowledge an AI game-design assistant retrieves while it interviews a creator, proposes options, prototypes and builds an XR game (Quest mixed reality or PC). Everything on this page is read from the files in this repository.

How the knowledge base is built and used

1 SOURCES13 documents + sessionsstudio GDDs, engine docs, venue catalogue, design literature notes, design conversations
2 CHUNKS581 chunks320 tokens, 40 overlap, embedded with embeddinggemma-300m (raw layer, not published)
3 CARDS1,025 cardsdistilled by an LLM with code checks: title, text, use_when, do, avoid, tags, source
4a FACTS → RULES92 fact rulesengine limits, defaults, genre rules: matched on the brief by structured triggers, not by vectors
4b VECTORSdense + tag booststhe rest of the cards, max 3 per category, 6 or 9 per step

These 92 cards are hard facts: what the engines can and cannot build, defaults, and rules of each genre. Similar-sounding text is not a good signal for them, so they leave the vector search and fire on the brief through structured triggers (lecture: not every question belongs in a vector database). At most 3 fire per step, highest priority first.

Does the right card reach the design AI? A card-level retrieval test on 36 design steps from 18 recorded design sessions (simulated creators), all four phases. Every method's top 15 cards were pooled and graded blind (0 not useful, 1 helpful, 2 essential). k = what the live system sends: 6 or 9 cards.

One shared template for describing a whole game. Unity and Unreal store a game in different folders and file types, but both hold the same parts. The template takes that shared skeleton (12 sections, 49 slots), adds the design intent that only a design document holds, and is filled from each design. One filled template = one game; a blank slot is something the builder would have to guess. template/game-template.json

What it shows

  • Rules, player, levels and design intent are almost always filled. These are what the interview asks about and what the knowledge base is richest in.
  • Audio and animation are mostly blank (music, sound effects and character animation missing in 50 to 90% of designs), and they are also where the knowledge base is thinnest: 1 to 4 cards each. The template shows the same gap from the design side that the Gaps tab shows from the needs side.
  • The knowledge base does not make designs more complete. With it, 15.3% of slots are missing; without, 14.7% (fewer blanks in 3 of 10 briefs, sign test p = 0.34). It changes which slots get filled: more opponents, HUD and allies; fewer input devices and player views. It made designs better (With vs without tab), not fuller.
  • Next: write cards for the empty slots, ask the interview about them, and retrieve cards by template slot so each blank gets its own knowledge.
